Checked: for all three decks (s1, s2, s3), the full text in `.review/day4-text/`: every slide's visible text and speaker notes, and all 21 practice prompts in full. I also read the help, prompt-list and prompt-dialog text in each deck's script, the s3 deployment card and prompt-pack header, `AGENTS.md`, the Day 4 Session 2 spec, and the headline outline for s1. I viewed these screenshots: s1 s03–s18 and p04; s2 s04–s08, s10, s12–s16 and s18; s3 s02–s14 and s16. Outside sources I checked on 2026-11-17:
- The live Frankfurter endpoint (`https://api.frankfurter.dev/v2/rate/usd/idr` returned `{"date":"2026-11-17","base":"USD","quote":"IDR","rate":18245}`).
- The google-drive-mcp authentication docs (raw GitHub `docs/authentication.md`).

Not checked:

- I did not open s1 s01–s02, s2 s01–s03, s09, s11 and s17, or s3 s01 and s15 as images. I read their text only, and none of my findings rests on them.
- The OpenCode permission defaults are from today's docs; the version installed in class may behave differently.
- I did not read the other reviewers' files.

## Verdict per deck

- **s1:** The exchange-rate material is accurate and carefully hedged. The endpoint, the four fields, the division direction, and the fresh, cached and unavailable states all check out against the live service, and the prompts teach good habits (no made-up fallback rate, tests with fake responses kept apart from a live check). The weak point is the Drive half. Three slide titles promise that the agent acts only after you approve, but that holds only if the agent obeys the prompt. The OpenCode default lets it call the Drive tool, with full read and write access to the whole Drive, without asking. The warning that file contents go to the model provider sits only in the notes and help.
- **s2:** The core lesson is right and well protected: login answers who you are, roles answer what you may do, the back end checks the role, and hiding the menu protects nothing. The audit rules are sound too. Problems:
  - "Added, never edited" overstates what a training audit table guarantees.
  - The LDAP notes overstate how small a real directory swap would be.
  - The prompts and Session 3 depend on CSRF protection and a failed-sign-in limit that no slide teaches.
- **s3:** The most technically careful deck: SQLite backup through the backup API, files 0700 and 0600, lingering, `example.invalid`, the publication gates and changing the published practice password are all correct. Its accuracy risks are in the pictures. Slide 4 does not say which of its two bottom boxes is wrong, and slide 5 draws the price service as a browser pushing data in. The prompts also require two things the slides never set up: checking the SSH host key, and an LLM key whose source is never named.

## What works well

- **s1 slides 7, 9 and p01/p03:** The conversion and failure material is accurate and teaches a strong bank habit. The direction of the division is checked by units ("IDR divided by IDR per USD produces USD"). There is "never an invented rate": a missing rate shows IDR only. Cached rates are labelled, and repeatable tests with fake responses are kept apart from a live check. The live endpoint returned exactly the four fields slide 6 shows.
- **s2 slides 9, 10, 15:** The quiz, then the back-end check, then proof with a direct request (403) teaches the right model: hiding a menu is tidiness, not protection. p03 and p06 test it with a direct request rather than by looking at the screen.
- **s2 slide 13 and p05:** The audit rules are correct and careful. Passwords, session passes, cookies and request bodies are never recorded, and the notes add that audit records themselves hold personal data.
- **s3 slide 6 and p04/p05:** The training password published in s2 is treated as burned before going public: unique passwords, old sessions cancelled, and publication blocked until the session protections pass, with "HTTPS alone is not sufficient" stated plainly.
- **s3 slides 7, 9, 14 notes and p03/p07:** The data-safety technique is accurate: the SQLite backup API instead of copying a live database file, 0700/0600 permissions, state kept outside code releases, test restores to a separate path, and code rollback kept apart from data recovery.
