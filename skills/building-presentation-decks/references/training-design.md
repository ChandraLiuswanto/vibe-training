# Training-deck design

These rules come from multimedia-learning research (Mayer) and retrieval-practice studies. Each rule maps to a slide pattern.

## Session skeleton (repeat per chunk of 8-15 minutes)

| Slide | Purpose | Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Hook | Why care | A concrete problem in the learner's own world ("Budget_v2_FINAL_really.xlsx"), stated as a question or a pain point |
| Key idea | One concept | One sentence + one everyday analogy + one diagram built up click by click |
| Worked example | Show it done | Input → what happened → result. Fade the support over the session: full example first, then a template with blanks, then only the goal |
| Your turn | Do it | Exact task, the time box, and "you're done when…" criteria |
| Check | Retrieve | 1-2 multiple-choice questions with link-based feedback that explains *why* |

Bookends:
- **Opening:** objectives in learner language, plus an agenda hub whose items link to each section.
- **Start of each section/day:** 1-2 recap questions on earlier material (spaced retrieval beats re-reading).
- **Close:** revisit each objective as "you can now…". Add a cheat sheet and next steps.

## Analogies and misconceptions

For every new term, give an analogy from the audience's daily work. Name the most common misconception and correct it on the slide or in the notes. Example: "commit ≠ sharing: a commit saves only on your computer until you push."

Introduce key terms *before* the process that uses them (a mini glossary slide). That keeps the process slide down to one new idea.

## Quiz pattern (link-based, works everywhere)

```
Quiz slide (lock_click_advance)
  answer A ──link──▶ hidden "Not quite: <why>" slide ──[Try again]──▶ quiz
  answer B ──link──▶ hidden "Correct: <why>" slide   ──[Continue]───▶ next content slide
```
- Write distractors from real misconceptions, not throwaway options.
- Feedback explains the reasoning in one or two lines.
- Put the quiz right after its chunk, never all quizzes at the end.

## Animation rules for learning

- Animate only to **segment** (reveal steps in speaking order) or to **signal** (highlight the active step). Decorative motion measurably lowers recall (seductive-details effect).
- Keep labels on the diagram, not in a legend. Don't put on screen the paragraph the presenter will say aloud; it belongs in the notes.
- To show a process advancing, dim or recolor finished steps on a duplicate slide plus a Morph. Don't add more objects.
